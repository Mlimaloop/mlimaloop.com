The problem is not demand

A continent drowning in its own plastic.

East Africa generates millions of tonnes of plastic waste a year and almost none of it comes back. It chokes waterways, cities and soil — while the world's brands are paying a premium for exactly this material, certified and traceable.

01The collector

It starts with one bottle, and one person.

A collector picks a bottle off a roadside in Arusha, scans it into the app, drops it at a mobile buying station, and is paid on the spot. Every time. No middleman, no waiting, no discretion.

  • $0.165 / kg, paid instantly through the app to mobile money.
  • A dedicated collector earns around $210 a month — roughly 3× Tanzania's minimum wage.
  • Every kilo GPS-stamped and named: the chain of custody that unlocks premium ocean-bound plastic credits.
A collector logs a pickup · Arusha

The technology

One product. Three views of the same record.

LOOP is the street-facing brand collectors actually use. Every weigh-in lands once and is read three ways — as a collector's earnings, as a depot officer's queue and batch, and as an investor's provenance trail. Same data, same ground, different density.

  • Collector — paid to M-Pesa at the scale; red is what you collected, blue is what your team collected.
  • Officer — certified scale, GPS, reference and batch code captured at the point of weigh-in.
  • Investor — 98.2% traceable, every kilo attributed to a named collector, a certified scale and a timestamp.

02The network

Bring the depot to the community.

Five vehicles run scheduled mobile buying stations today, so collectors never travel far — scaling toward a fleet of 120 as sites roll out. At every visit they sell a basket of materials — PET, HDPE, aluminium, cardboard — at published, honest prices.

The surplus is already growing.

Feedstock is being collected and stored on site right now — bought at $0.165/kg, already worth $0.40/kg at today's market price. The stockpile grows every day: an appreciating asset on the balance sheet before the plant switches on.

Inside the plant

From bottle to food-grade pellet.

Because feedstock stockpiles during build-out, the plant runs two shifts from day one — around 7–8 tonnes processed daily. Six stages, fully traceable, through to a food-grade reactor and a pilot yarn line.

The loop closes

Nothing is wasted.

The bottle becomes food-grade pellet and recycled fibre. What cannot be recycled becomes something else entirely — and the value keeps circulating in the community that collected it.

rPET

Food-grade pellet and yarn for global brands.

HDPE

Jerricans and containers as a second product line.

Bricks

Non-recyclable plastic plus sand, pressed into interlocking bricks.

Roofing

Recycled composite sheets that outlast corrugated iron.

Why it holds

Every loop closed changes a life.

MlimaLoop is not a plant with a community programme bolted on. The community is the business — its collectors, its operators, its pride. As the network grows, so do the people and the cities around it.

0

Skilled plant jobs

42 fair, safe jobs per site — managers, operators, QC, drivers, security — across the network at full scale.

0

Collector livelihoods

Informal collectors formalised, paid fairly and on time, building loyal income that spreads through tight communities.

0

Plastic diverted

Tonnes kept out of landfill, rivers and the Indian Ocean over 15 years — the Pangani basin cleaned at source.

The waste becomes the bricks. The bricks become the homes. The homes keep the collectors who brought the waste in.

Worker housing built from recycled bricks. School chairs and desks from bottle caps. A formal economy where there was none.

The roll-out

Prove Arusha. Then replicate a nation.

One proven model, rolled out city by city. Arusha is the blueprint; every site after it is a copy of a system that already works — reaching 12 operating sites across Tanzania.

The economics

A business that funds its own expansion.

After Arusha is proven, each new site is paid for out of cash flow. The investor funds the first plant; the model does the rest.
